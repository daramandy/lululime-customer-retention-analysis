# Lululime: Customer Retention & Purchase Behavior Analysis
Dataverse 2026 UBC (Women in Data Science x Undergraduate Statistics Society) | Analyzing customer retention, purchasing behavior, and loyalty through statistical modeling and machine learning.

Team: LockedIn

# Project Background
Lululime is a Vancouver-based athleisure store facing growing competition from a nearby retailer. To keep customers coming back, the store introduced several initiatives, including a loyalty program, a Pilates marketing campaign, and a free repair service.

For Dataverse 2026, our team worked on a case study where we had to look at Lululime's customer data, evaluate its business performance, and help the company decide what to do with its loyalty and repair programs.

# My Analysis: Customer Retention & Repeat Purchasing
For my part of the project, I wanted to answer one question:
1. Who are Lululime's customers, and what makes them more likely to come back?

I started by exploring customer demographics, acquisition channels, and purchasing patterns using Python. From there, I used RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) analysis to better understand customer behavior and identify different customer segments.

I also built logistic regression and gradient boosting models to predict whether a customer would make another purchase within 90 days. To explore which factors were associated with repeat purchasing, I used multivariable logistic regression and statistical hypothesis testing.

# Key Findings
One of the most interesting things I found was that how recently and how often customers purchased mattered more than how much they had spent in the past.

Age was also significantly associated with repeat purchasing, with customers, aged 25 and above having higher odds of returning compared to those aged 18-24. On the other hand, acquisition channel and historical spending were not statistically significant after accounting for the other variables in the model.

# Takeaway
This analysis helped me understand how customer data can be used to identify purchasing patters and explore customer retention. It also showed that a customer who spends a lot is not necessarily the same as customer who will keep coming back.

The findings suggest that Lululime could benefit from paying more attention to customers' recent purchasing activity and shopping frequency when planning its retention strategies.
